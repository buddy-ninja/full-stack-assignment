# Heatstroke

Pets cannot sweat as well as people. Heatstroke can develop in minutes in a hot car, during exercise on a warm day, or in a hot room.

**Signs:** heavy panting, drooling, bright red gums, wobbliness, vomiting, collapse.

**What to do now:**

1. Move the pet to shade or a cool room.
2. Pour cool (not ice-cold) water over the body and use a fan.
3. **Contact a vet now**, even if the pet seems to recover. Organ damage can show up later.

Flat-faced breeds, overweight pets and older pets are at higher risk. Walk dogs early or late on hot days, and never leave a pet in a parked car.
