# Breathing difficulty

Trouble breathing is always an emergency. **Contact a vet now** if your pet:

- breathes with its mouth open (a cat panting is never normal)
- has blue, grey or very pale gums or tongue
- makes loud noises when breathing in, or struggles to breathe
- stretches its neck out or sits with elbows apart to breathe
- breathes very fast while resting or asleep

**Resting breathing rate:** a calm, sleeping dog or cat usually takes fewer than 30 breaths a minute. Count the rises of the chest for 30 seconds and double it. A rate that is higher than this and climbing is a reason to call a vet, even if the pet seems fine otherwise.

Keep the pet calm and cool on the way to the vet. Do not restrict the neck or chest.
