# Common household toxins

Some everyday items are dangerous even in small amounts. **If you know or suspect your pet ate one of these, contact a vet now.** Do not wait for symptoms, and do not try to make your pet vomit unless a vet tells you to.

| Toxin | Main risk |
|---|---|
| Chocolate, especially dark | Dogs: vomiting, racing heart, seizures |
| Xylitol (sugar-free gum, some peanut butters) | Dogs: very low blood sugar, liver damage |
| Grapes and raisins | Dogs: kidney failure |
| Lilies (all parts, even pollen or vase water) | Cats: kidney failure |
| Onions, garlic, chives | Damage to red blood cells |
| Rat and mouse poison | Bleeding or seizures, sometimes days later |
| Human painkillers (ibuprofen, paracetamol, aspirin) | Dogs and cats: stomach ulcers, kidney or liver damage. Paracetamol is especially dangerous for cats. |

**Never give human medication to a pet unless a vet has prescribed it.**

Bring the packaging with you if you can. It tells the vet what was eaten and how much.
